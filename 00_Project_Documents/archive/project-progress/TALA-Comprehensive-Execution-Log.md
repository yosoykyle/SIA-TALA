## Purpose and Reading Rules

This standalone record explains how the T.A.L.A. research, requirements, system design, construction, verification, and remaining validation work developed over time. It can be read without access to a source-code repository, task-management system, or separate tracking document. Each work package is described in reader-facing language, while normalized TAL identifiers remain optional traceability labels.

The table is a chronological record of work packages, not a list of invented Scrum sprints or retrospectively numbered cycles. Iterative and Incremental Development does not require one universal cycle length, phase vocabulary, or tabular template. Therefore, the record identifies the actual IID activity demonstrated by the chronology instead of assigning a cycle number that the team did not formally use at the time.

The following distinctions govern the record:

- An **increment** is a new or expanded working capability added to the evolving system, such as applicant intake, enrollment gates, payment reconciliation, grade release, or schedule publication.
- An **iteration** occurs when an earlier research conclusion, requirement, diagram, FS/TS statement, PRD rule, architecture decision, implementation, or test baseline is re-examined and changed using consultation, review findings, verification results, provider behavior, or a newly discovered constraint.
- **Verification** checks whether an artifact or implementation meets its stated requirements. Testing alone is not automatically an iteration. It becomes part of an iteration only when its findings cause an earlier baseline to be corrected and reverified.
- **Stakeholder validation** occurs when the client or panel determines whether the integrated system satisfies its intended institutional use. Developer verification is not reported as stakeholder validation.
- Research work is included when it established requirements, changed system scope or technology selection, produced a lifecycle artifact, or documented a material system decision. Pure formatting, forwarding, academic-break, and submission activities are identified as parallel research documentation rather than software increments.
- FS/TS and PRD dates represent requirements and system-design work. They do not by themselves prove that corresponding software was completed on the same date.
- Recorded periods are evidence-supported calendar windows, not person-hours. When development, verification, correction, and reverification occurred within one recorded day, the table does not invent unsupported hour-by-hour sequencing.
- Planned and future tasks are included for lifecycle completeness, but they do not count as evidence that an increment, iteration, validation, or delivery has already occurred.
- The task sequence is normalized as TAL-1 through TAL-190. Consecutive identifiers with the same status are compressed using inclusive ranges. For example, `TAL-16–27, 29–30, 34–40` excludes TAL-28 and TAL-31–33. No range crosses a completed, canceled, in-progress, planned, or future status boundary.

## SDLC Determination and Defense

T.A.L.A. is best classified as **Iterative and Incremental Development**. The IEEE Computer Society describes iterative development as repeated development and refinement and incremental development as the successive addition of functionality. Recognized IID implementations vary in iteration length, time-boxing, and the amount of initial specification work; their common characteristic is that the product is not produced through one irreversible sequential pass (Larman & Basili, 2003; IEEE Computer Society, 2025).

The completed project history is **iterative** because earlier work was repeatedly reopened and improved. Client needs were refined through research and observation; the Functional and Technical Specifications underwent 34 paired revisions; use cases, DFDs, the ERD, questionnaires, and manuscript sections were reviewed and corrected; the proposed automated document-extraction integration was replaced by OR-Tools CP-SAT scheduling; the large FS/TS baseline was reduced into 13 modular PRDs; and developer verification later caused corrections to access rules, enrollment gates, official outputs, obsolete paths, solver validation, scheduling recovery, provider compatibility, and shared-cohort conflict handling.

The completed project history is **incremental** because working capabilities accumulated in identifiable functional groups: project and database foundations; identity and role access; admissions and student records; academic setup; term offerings and scheduling; enrollment; finance and payments; official outputs; grades and lifecycle; Student Hub; reporting, audit, imports, and monitoring; CP-SAT integration; and PayMongo integration. These capabilities were constructed and integrated into one evolving application rather than delivered as unrelated systems.

The delivery pattern is **Incremental Development–Single Delivery**. NASA uses this term for separately developed increments that are integrated and delivered as one system (National Aeronautics and Space Administration, 2004). T.A.L.A. did not obtain recurring client acceptance or make a stakeholder delivery after every increment. Developer verification occurred throughout construction, while the rehearsal and formal client/panel review remain the validation boundary before one integrated delivery. ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 also permits life-cycle processes to be applied iteratively, recursively, concurrently, and incrementally rather than requiring one universal sequence of named phases (International Organization for Standardization et al., 2026).

The classification is based on completed historical behavior, not merely on task titles or future intentions. Upcoming regression and usability work may create further iterations if findings cause controlled corrections. Future records, privacy, messaging, and audit improvements would be new increments only if they are later approved and implemented. Until then, they remain planned or future work and are not counted as delivered evidence.

The record does not support describing the project as Scrum, Rapid Application Development (RAD), Rational Unified Process (RUP), or Waterfall. The evidence does not establish Scrum roles and recurring events, recurring user-tested RAD prototypes, or deliberate governance through RUP phases. Conversely, the repeated reopening of requirements, design, implementation, and verification contradicts a strict single-pass Waterfall interpretation (Agile Manifesto authors, 2001; IBM, n.d.; Stryker, 2026).

## Current Lifecycle Position

As of July 18, 2026, T.A.L.A. has completed requirements discovery, 34 paired Functional and Technical Specification revisions, a major OCR-to-CP-SAT requirements and architecture rebaseline, conversion of the large specifications into 13 modular PRDs, numerous vertical functional increments, two external-integration increments, and extensive developer verification and rework. The normalized TAL-1–TAL-190 work corpus contains **172 completed tasks, 10 canceled tasks, one in-progress task, three planned tasks, and four future nonblocking tasks**. The cancellations remain visible as scope removals, replacements, decompositions, or deferrals rather than being hidden or reported as delivered functionality.

The table demonstrates the two dimensions of IID without relying on artificial cycle numbering. New or expanded working capabilities are labeled **increments**. A requirement, design, PRD, implementation, or test baseline is labeled an **iteration** only when the record shows that it was reopened and changed. Verification is separately identified, and a correction is followed by reverification when the chronology supports that sequence.

Construction and developer verification are substantially advanced, but the lifecycle is not complete. Shared-cohort scheduling correction and replacement evidence remain in progress. PayMongo demonstration readiness, system-wide cross-role regression, evidence-based usability correction, the final user manual, rehearsal, client/panel validation, and single integrated delivery remain pending. Consequently, T.A.L.A. must not yet be described as stakeholder-validated, accepted, deployed for institutional operation, or delivered.
