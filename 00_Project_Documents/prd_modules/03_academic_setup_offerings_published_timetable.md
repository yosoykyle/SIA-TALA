# PRD 03 — Academic Setup, Offerings, and Published Timetable
## Authority

This PRD is the complete authority for academic setup, curricula, Terms, cohorts, Class Offerings, teaching-resource readiness, whole-term CP-SAT generation, candidate review, publication, and revision. It is understandable without legacy scheduling PRDs, solver source, tests, or the formulation document. The current implementation remains bounded evidence until a later PRD 03 vertical slice proves conformance.
## 1. Purpose and Successful Outcome

Clinic 3 defines one complete institutional journey:

`Programs and subjects → active curriculum → active term calendar → confirmed cohorts and classes → teaching-resource readiness → whole-term CP-SAT generation → human review → published timetable → controlled revisions`

The successful outcome is one immutable published timetable version that every authorized role reads through a purpose-specific projection. Candidate schedules are proposals. Only a Registrar-published version is official.

Clinic 3 does not own admissions, student-level study planning, enrollment, seat reservation, finance, COR, grades, internship operations, or Student activation.

Term Cohorts supply planning groups and class associations. Optional individual student-section membership belongs to PRD 04's term Enrollment; it is explicitly recorded and must not be inferred from the classes serving a planning group. Existing forecast/confirmed counts retain their planning meaning. Membership administration alone never changes solver groups, class assignments or the published timetable.

## 4. End-to-End Narrative

1. Registrar records each offered Program (code, name, length).
2. Registrar prepares a Draft curriculum manually or copies an existing plan, including any external qualifications it names, resolves every blocking finding, and activates the immutable version in one confirmation (finalizing ready draft subject revisions).
3. Registrar explicitly creates a Draft Term Calendar Package, records its approved dates and teaching grid, resolves failed readiness checks, and activates it.
4. TALA projects continuing-student demand, Clinic 2 Ready-for-Enrollment counts, and bounded Clinic 4 unmet-demand evidence. Registrar confirms or splits standard-curriculum cohorts. TALA may generate Draft Class Offerings from those authoritative inputs; Registrar confirms, shares, adds, or cancels them.
5. Registrar records Faculty eligibility and term capacity decisions, rooms, genuine hard unavailability, and any authorized exact commitments.
6. Readiness derives whether every confirmed Class Offering that needs recurring meetings has complete, consistent scheduling inputs.
7. One immutable whole-term snapshot is submitted to CP-SAT. Only one run may be active for the term.
8. Laravel independently revalidates every returned assignment and records the result with factual quality and diagnostic evidence.
9. Registrar reviews the complete candidate and may make bounded, fully revalidated corrections. Hard rules cannot be waived.
10. Any institutional sign-off occurs outside TALA. Registrar publishes the first immutable timetable version with automatic actor/time attribution; a routine approval reference/date is optional. Required policy/source evidence and genuine exceptional authorizations remain mandatory.
11. Faculty sees assigned official meetings. Students see only the meetings for their Clinic 4 placements after official enrollment.
12. A post-publication change creates a Draft revision, revalidates the entire timetable, publishes a new immutable version, and preserves the superseded version and exact impact.

## 5. Catalog and Curriculum Authority

### 5.1 Programs, Courses, and revisions

A `Program` records the degree or diploma: code, name, length in years and whether it is offered. Program recognition and curriculum approval documents stay with the school and regulator; TALA does not record approval periods, issuing offices or approval references, and no Catalog step waits on them. (Owner decision, 18 November 2026: Servitech does not use these records; they were removed to keep the workflow understandable.)

#### Program lifecycle, Admissions availability, and deactivation guard

A `Program` operates in an `Active` (`is_active = true`) or `Inactive` (`is_active = false`) state:

- **2-condition Program Availability in Admissions:** A Program is available to applicants in an Admission Cycle if and only if **both** conditions hold: (1) `Program.is_active` is `true`, **and** (2) the incoming batch Academic Year is assigned to a published (`Active` or `Retired`) curriculum for that Program. When either condition is unmet, TALA surfaces the exact reason (e.g., `"Program is inactive"` or `"Not available in Admissions: no curriculum for Batch YYYY–YY"`).
- **Deactivation scope (CHED teach-out protection):** Deactivating a Program stops new student intakes, new Admission Cycle attachments, and new curriculum/batch additions while preserving continuing batches, existing published curricula, and currently enrolled students so teach-out continues uninterrupted.
- **Refined hard deactivation guard:** A Program cannot be deactivated when **either**:
  1. An open published Admission Cycle has unresolved applicants (`AdmissionApplication` not `Withdrawn` or `NotAdmitted`) for that Program, **or**
  2. The Program is the last remaining active Program attached to an open published Admission Cycle.
  In either case, Registrar must first resolve/close the affected cycle or ensure another active Program covers the open cycle.
- **Impact preview:** The deactivation confirmation modal displays live impact counts for continuing batches, enrolled students, active curricula, and open applications before confirmation.
- **Deletion vs. permanent historical retention:** Brand-new 0-record typo Programs (with zero curricula, students, cohorts, sections, or applications) may still be hard-deleted. Programs with any historical records are never deleted; once continuing batches finish, an inactive Program remains **`"Inactive, no students"`** permanently unless reactivated.
- **Reactivation & `Activate and proceed` prompt:** Authorized Registrar staff may reactivate an `Inactive` Program directly. Attempting to add a batch, create a new curriculum, or continue editing a draft curriculum on an `Inactive` Program triggers an explicit **`Activate and proceed`** confirmation that reactivates the Program before continuing (deleting an unneeded draft remains allowed while the Program is inactive).
- **Published curriculum immutability:** Published (`Active` and `Retired`) curricula remain locked and immutable once activated; they are never edited in place.

One stable `Course` owns course identity and code. An immutable `CourseRevision` (`CourseSpecification`) owns the approved title, units, Philippine CHED/TESDA-aligned academic classification (`CourseAcademicClassification`: `ORDINARY` and `TESDA_COMPETENCY` included in GWA; `PE`, `NSTP`, and `NON_CREDIT` / Bridging excluded from GWA), prerequisites, corequisites, equivalencies, contact requirements, allowed delivery, room requirements, and effective use.

- Prerequisites and corequisites use separate required-course lists. A prerequisite requires an earlier released satisfying result or exact approved credit. A corequisite permits that prior satisfaction or valid concurrent enrollment in the same Term; PRD 04 validates the complete selection and placement together.
- An approved equivalent may satisfy a requisite.
- No nested Boolean rule builder or generic policy language exists.
- **Direct subject revision editing & Draft lifecycle:** Editing an `Active` subject opens the edit form directly, creating or updating a single `Draft` revision rather than spawning duplicate blank drafts. Before activation, the `Draft` revision surfaces a concise diff of changes against the current active revision and permits discarding an unreferenced `Draft` revision cleanly.
- **Active revision immutability & historical clarity:** An `Active` or historically used revision is read-only. Change history clearly distinguishes the newest **`Current`** active revision from earlier active revisions retained for historical curricula.
- **Draft curriculum fork-on-save:** Editing a shared or non-draft `CourseSpecification` inside a Draft curriculum opens teaching/schedule fields directly with *"Draft-only changes"* guidance and atomically forks a new `DRAFT` revision on save only when teaching fields actually change (preserving requisites and repointing only the draft entry; pure placement moves do not fork), so published curricula and their enrolled batches remain strictly immutable.
- Matching text or similar codes never establish course identity or equivalency.

### 5.2 Curriculum Versions and Batch Assignment

`CurriculumVersion` groups Course Revisions by curriculum year and term and maps explicitly to one or more intake Academic Years through **`curriculum_version_batches`**. Activation records who activated it and when.

#### Explicit batch-to-curriculum assignment

- **One published curriculum per intake Academic Year per Program:** Within a Program, each Academic Year batch may belong to at most one published curriculum (`curriculum_version_batches` enforces uniqueness per Program + Academic Year and per Curriculum + Academic Year).
- **Auto-generated batch range labels:** Curriculum display labels automatically collapse contiguous assigned school years and preserve gap years (e.g., `Batch 2027–28 to 2029–30, 2032–33`), falling back to the recorded name/code only when no batch is assigned yet.
- **`Add batch` without copying subjects:** Registrar can assign an additional available incoming Academic Year directly to an `Active` curriculum via **`Add batch`** without duplicating subjects or creating a new curriculum version (subject to the **`Activate and proceed`** prompt if the Program is `Inactive`).
- **`Remove batch` guard:** Removing an assigned batch from a curriculum is permitted **only when 0 students (`StudentProfile`), 0 cohorts (`TermCohort`), and 0 unresolved applications (`AdmissionApplication`) exist** for that Program and Academic Year.

#### Curriculum states (`Draft`, `Active`, `Retired`) and continuing batches

`CurriculumVersion` uses three canonical lifecycle states (`DRAFT` → **`Draft`**, `ACTIVE` → **`Active`**, and `SUPERSEDED` → **`Retired`**):

- **`Draft`:** Editable study plan (created blank or copied from an `Active` or `Retired` curriculum with `copied_from_curriculum_version_id` lineage tracking `Copied`, `Edited`, `Added`, and `Removed` entries against the source). Not consumable by Admissions, Term Planning, Enrollment, Grades, or Completion until published.
- **`Active`:** Published, immutable curriculum available for its assigned intake batches and open to receiving additional future batches via `Add batch`. Multiple batched curricula may remain `Active` concurrently for distinct intake Academic Years; activating a batched Draft does not auto-supersede other `Active` curricula for other batches (legacy unbatched activation retains single-active supersession).
- **`Retired` (`SUPERSEDED`):** Explicitly retired from `Active` via **Retire** (or superseded under legacy unbatched activation) so no new batches can be added. **Continuing batches assigned to a `Retired` curriculum remain fully operable** across Term Planning (cohorts, class proposals, class offerings), Enrollment (batch-aware standard registration), Grades, and Completion (curriculum evaluation and external competency checks). A `Retired` curriculum remains read-only and may still be copied into a new `Draft`.

Registrar activates a complete Draft in one confirmation (with `Activate and proceed` if the Program is `Inactive`). There is no separate approval step and no Academic Head or committee approval queue. The year-by-year curriculum stepper (`1st Year → … → Nth Year → Review`) is the primary work surface:

- Draft rows may be edited; editing a shared subject in a Draft curriculum opens teaching/schedule fields directly with clear *"Draft-only changes"* guidance and auto-forks a Draft `CourseSpecification` atomically on save only when teaching fields actually change (pure placement moves do not fork), so source published curricula remain strictly unchanged.
- `Active` and `Retired` rows are strictly read-only.
- Manual subject preparation (including fast inline Quick-Add with code auto-fill and catalog picking), curriculum copying, and 3-step spreadsheet import (`.xlsx`/`.csv`) create or populate Draft records without overwriting existing published specifications.
- Creating or copying a plan never activates it or overwrites an authoritative published record.
- **Curriculum prospectus uniqueness vs. retakes and student classifications:** Each canonical `Course` may appear **at most once per `CurriculumVersion`**. A curriculum prospectus is the Program's authoritative graduation checklist; repeating a subject across year levels or semesters would double-count required units and corrupt degree-progress calculations. Retakes, back-subjects, transferees, shiftees, returnees, and mixed-year irregular enrollments are served by Term Planning `ClassOffering` / `Additional` classes (§7) and PRD 04 `IndividuallyAdvised` registration alongside credit/equivalency evaluation — never by duplicating a subject across year levels in a curriculum.
- Duplicate course placements within the same curriculum, invalid units (`<= 0`), empty drafts (0 subjects total), cyclic prerequisites, malformed or unresolved requisite links, inconsistent titles, differing units, missing references, and invalid year/term placement remain blocking findings until resolved. Reciprocal corequisites form a concurrent group and remain distinct from prerequisite cycles. Empty individual semesters surface non-blocking advisory notices so valid short-term or single-semester plans remain publishable.
- Grades, remarks, and student outcomes in evaluation spreadsheets remain student-record evidence and never become catalog fields.

#### Manual curriculum preparation

Registrar prepares a curriculum in its selected Program. The year-by-year stepper supports inline Quick-Add (auto-filling existing subject defaults by code and assigning a CHED/CP-SAT Room/Schedule preset), adding existing approved or Draft subject revisions from the Subject Catalog, editing Draft placement and detailed teaching/requisite configuration directly (with *"Draft-only changes"* guidance and atomic fork-on-save only when teaching fields actually change; pure placement moves do not fork), and copying an existing `Active` or `Retired` curriculum to a successor Draft. Creating subjects and placing them in the selected Draft completes atomically; failure leaves neither partial subjects nor partial placements. Validate current Registrar permission and Draft state at submission. The subject identity and its versioned academic definition remain distinct records; their creation is one understandable task.

Year level and semester describe positions in the reusable study plan. Assigned intake batches (`curriculum_version_batches`) and the optional effective entry Term identify which dated student cohorts follow the plan. Actual teaching dates and offered classes belong to Term Planning. Ordinary semester labels and a new row's display order derive from its chosen group; preserve explicit historical source labels and existing order when editing unrelated facts.

#### Draft-only spreadsheet import and canonical export (`.xlsx` / `.csv`)

Registrar may also prepare catalog subjects (`Subject catalog` → **Import subjects**) or populate an open `Draft` curriculum (`ReviewCurriculumVersion` → **Import from spreadsheet**) through a stateless 3-step `.xlsx`/`.csv` wizard (**Upload file & worksheet selection** with downloadable `.xlsx`/`.csv` templates → **Match columns** with Servitech header aliases and auto-detected Year/Semester section banners → **Preview & import** with inline fixes for blocked rows, optional skip-blocked toggle, and formula-safe skipped-rows CSV download). Importing into a curriculum is restricted strictly to `Draft` curricula (`Active` and `Retired` curricula never accept imports), links existing catalog subjects without overwriting their `Active` specifications, derives Lecture/Laboratory components and `Externally arranged` practicum treatment from weekly hours or practicum notations, resolves prerequisites across the catalog and uploaded batch (warning on self-prerequisites or non-subject notes without blocking valid rows), and skips summary/formula/bridging/signature rows. Both `Subject catalog` and `ReviewCurriculumVersion` (`Draft`, `Active`, and `Retired`) support formula-safe `.xlsx` and `.csv` export for round-trip curriculum preparation and offline review. Standalone persistent import-history queues and separate approval/posting steps remain retired.

#### CHED MORPHE alignment and optional TESDA external certification tracking

- **CHED MORPHE alignment & `CourseAcademicClassification`:** Ordinary collegiate subjects use **`Weekly scheduled class`**, supporting `Lecture Room`, `Computer Lab`, and `Laboratory` room-type needs across 1–6+ units, Lecture+Lab splits (e.g., 2h Lecture + 3h Laboratory), Prerequisites, Corequisites, approved Equivalents, and the 5 Philippine CHED/TESDA-aligned academic classifications (`CourseAcademicClassification`):
  - **Included in GWA:** `ORDINARY` (Regular academic subject) and `TESDA_COMPETENCY` (TESDA / TVET Competency taught inside the program).
  - **Excluded from GWA:** `PE` (Physical Education), `NSTP` (CWTS / LTS / ROTC), and `NON_CREDIT` / Bridging.
- **TESDA-aligned subjects vs. external assessments:** `TESDA_COMPETENCY` subjects taught as part of a program are normal curriculum subjects carrying their recorded program/curriculum units and counting toward GWA. Optional external TESDA National Assessment / NC certification tracking (`ExternalCompetencyRequirement`) remains distinct and sits cleanly on the curriculum **Review** / summary step (rather than repeating empty panels across Year 1–N). Each recorded requirement owns only the qualification name, an optional level, an optional linked subject, its Curriculum Version, and treatment:

- `TrackedOnly` records an externally verified result for curriculum-evaluation visibility and never blocks enrollment, grades, completion, or conferral.
- `CompletionRequired` is chosen only when the curriculum explicitly makes the external result a graduation requirement.

The supplied Servitech evaluation forms establish that assessment dates and remarks are tracked; they do not by themselves authorize `CompletionRequired`. TALA does not infer a requirement from similar course titles, TESDA labels in schedule samples, or another institution's practice. The requirement is not a generic credential, certification, or curriculum-rule builder. TESDA assessment and certification remain external; Clinic 5 owns only the verified Student result and its approved curriculum projection.

### 5.3 Courses without recurring meetings

A Course Revision owns zero or more approved weekly meeting requirements.

- **`Weekly scheduled class`:** Ordinary lectures, computer labs, and laboratories use bounded recurring meeting blocks (supporting 1–6+ units, Lec+Lab splits, room types, the 5 `CourseAcademicClassification` categories, and requisites) scheduled by CP-SAT.
- **`Externally arranged` (OJT / practicum):** An internship or externally arranged practicum stays in the curriculum as a unit-bearing subject (counted in semester, year, and curriculum totals) and remains enrollable, COR-visible, gradable, and included in completion records without invented classroom meetings.
- A course with no genuine recurring meeting is labeled **Externally arranged — no class schedule** (no recurring master-timetable meeting) and receives no CP-SAT class slot.
- A genuine recurring seminar may have its own meeting block without scheduling all workplace hours.

Clinic 3 does not manage placement sites, companies, supervisors, workplace attendance, or practicum logbooks.

## 6. Term Calendar Package

Academic Head approves the institutional calendar outside TALA. Registrar records the authority and evidence, activates the package, and operates its windows. Academic Head receives read-only oversight. TALA adds no duplicate approval action.

Every First, Second, or institutionally approved Special Term begins through an explicitly created `TermCalendarPackage`. The additional concurrent-Term demonstration is deferred; exact-Term isolation remains required. More than one exact Term may be `Active` concurrently, including prior-term teaching or grade work while next-term registration or adjustment is open. The uniqueness boundary is one active package version per exact Term, not one active Term for the institution. Every window, class, timetable, registration, roster, result, account, COR, and output carries its exact Term; no action may infer one global current term. TALA never automatically creates or clones the next term, and `Special Term` is a controlled term type rather than a separate Summer subsystem.

### 6.1 State and owned records

Stored state is only the 3 canonical Term states:

- `Draft` — upcoming planning term (calendar, cohorts, classes, and teaching resources may be prepared before activation).
- `Active` — current operational term (active teaching calendar, scheduling, operational windows, and published timetable use).
- `Closed` — historical read-only archive (preserved for audit, academic-history, and historical schedule reference with no operational mutations).

Across Term Planning, those 3 stored states drive a **4-mode Term Lifecycle contract**:

1. **`Draft`** (`Term::StateDraft` with no activated calendar yet): full pre-publish setup; unused draft terms with zero offerings, cohorts, admission cycles, fee plans, enrollments, solver runs, timetable versions, or activated calendars may be permanently deleted via **`Delete draft term`** in the separated **Term lifecycle / Danger zone** inside `Term settings`.
2. **`Planning`** (active calendar or `Active` term before an official timetable is published): full pre-publish class preparation, faculty/room readiness, reminders, and candidate timetable generation/editing. Editing core calendar dates/hours marks active draft solver runs `Stale`.
3. **`Live / Published`** (has an active `PublishedTimetableVersion::StatePublished` timetable and is not `Closed`): pre-publish creation and mutation controls lock across `Calendar` (`Save & activate`), `Classes & sections` (`Add student section`, `Load from curriculum`, `Add class`, `Confirm`, `Edit class`, `Cancel class`), and `Faculty & rooms` (`Send reminder`, `Resend reminder`, `Room closures`), routing schedule changes to **`Timetable → Revise schedule`** (or **`Cancel class`**) and blocking full timetable regeneration while official student enrollments exist. Cross-term mutations in **School Resources** and **Faculty & rooms** block deactivating/shrinking rooms, unchecking qualified subjects, or lowering teaching-load limits below active official meetings across any non-closed term. Readiness evaluation scopes to confirmed active classes so unconfirmed draft leftovers do not trigger contradictory blockers, while 1-step `Update activity dates` remains available for operational windows.
4. **`Closed`** (`Term::StateClosed`): strictly read-only historical archive across all four planning stages, `Term settings`, `Timetable audit record` (`ViewScheduleGenerationRun`), and all backend scheduling services (`ScheduleGenerationService`, `ReviewTimetableCandidate`, `SchedulePublishService`, `PublishedScheduleRevisionService`, `RevertCandidateRun`, `ActivateTermCalendarPackage`). **`Close & archive term`** (Registrar-only in the separated **Term lifecycle / Danger zone** inside `Term settings`) hard-blocks while running solver jobs, draft timetable revisions, or open published admission cycles exist, and displays live counts of unreleased grade rosters and in-progress enrollments before transitioning the term into archive.

Activating a `TermCalendarPackage` (`ActivateTermCalendarPackage`) also synchronizes `Term.starts_on` and `Term.ends_on` to the activated package's `classes_start_on` and `classes_end_on` when present.

The package owns:

- Academic year, controlled term type, institution-approved display label, administrative dates, and class dates.
- Recorded calendar authority; routine approval reference/date are optional and Registrar actor/time are captured automatically. Genuine exceptional school authorizations retain their required evidence.
- For a Special Term, the approved particular schedule and attributable class-hour/class-day basis required by the recorded calendar authority.
- Typed operational windows with inclusive Asia/Manila close dates and an optional approved cutoff time.
- One weekly teaching-grid row for every allowed teaching day.
- Recurring institutional breaks.
- Dated holidays, no-class periods, make-up dates, and other approved exceptions.

No weekday, operating-hour, break, preferred-time, Special Term unit limit, or compressed-schedule value is assumed. Scheduling uses a fixed code-owned 30-minute grid inside the approved operating hours; the granularity is not configurable.

Application dates remain exclusively owned by Admission Cycles and are never duplicated into `term_calendar_windows`; Term Calendar displays any linked `AdmissionCycle` windows (name, Asia/Manila open/close dates, status badge, and direct `Manage in Admissions` link) as a compact read-only reference. Payment dates remain in accounts. The approved `Examination Period` is informational: its inclusive dates, Term display label, calendar authority, effective package version, owner, and as-of time are shown from the same calendar source as useful read-only information for Faculty and officially enrolled Students. Registrar edits the source; Academic Head retains source oversight. No redundant Examination Period record or projection is required. Exact class arrangements remain Faculty-owned and use the approved teaching channel. Missing or stale evidence shows **Examination period unavailable — contact Registrar or Faculty** and never derives a date from class meetings. Clinic 3 includes no class-level examination record or examination scheduler. The neutral `Enrollment` operational window owns approved opening and closing dates only. Clinic 4 assigns its bounded applicability to Ready Applicants, Standard continuing Students, Individually Advised or exception cases, or all otherwise eligible learners. The `Grade Entry` window owns the definite Faculty submission period and due date consumed by Clinic 5; Clinic 5 owns late-grade authority, INC deadlines, release, and correction behavior. No programmable audience rules are introduced. Core Term activation is independent of unrelated operational windows and the Faculty declaration deadline. Activating a term does not open enrollment; Clinic 4 also requires a published timetable and its remaining readiness facts.

## 7. Cohorts and Class Offerings

### 7.1 Term Cohorts

`TermCohort` represents a Registrar-confirmed standard-curriculum cohort, not a policy-driving Regular/Irregular Student classification. Forecast demand combines continuing-student data with Clinic 2 Ready-for-Enrollment counts. Registrar confirms the actual count and creates or splits cohorts. TALA does not automatically split cohorts using a universal target.

### 7.2 Class Offerings

`ClassOffering` is the actual class or section for the term.

Stored state is only `Draft`, `Confirmed`, or `Cancelled`. Blocked, ready for scheduling, candidate scheduled, and published are derived projections.

Each Class Offering records the Course Revision, stable class reference, `Regular` or `Additional` source, linked cohorts and expected count, authoritative institutional class capacity, weekly meeting pattern and per-block mode, room needs, applicable exact commitments, and current readiness. `Regular` describes a curriculum-planned offering source only; it is not a Student or enrollment status.

TALA provides one full curriculum-and-demand bulk preview from the active curriculum, confirmed cohorts, forecasts, and bounded `UnmetClassDemandProjection` evidence returned by Clinic 4. Registrar reviews the complete proposed class set, handles splits, shares, additions and cancellations, and confirms the valid set together. Preview creates no confirmed classes; confirmation rechecks the existing class-confirmation source versions, academic authority, demand, class capacity and meeting definition guards. Class/resource preparation remains possible in parallel. Faculty eligibility, declarations, resource preparation and coherent aggregate Faculty load/preparation limits are checked at their applicable generation-readiness stage; they create no class/availability request cycle. CP-SAT schedules the whole confirmed term and never creates or merges offerings; independent validation and truthful solver outcomes/metrics remain required.

One `Additional` Class Offering covers an externally approved retake, catch-up, elective, or other exceptional class. TALA does not create separate petition, tutorial, summer, or special-offering workflow engines.

Compatible cohorts may share one Class Offering only for the same canonical Course or an approved equivalency. Registrar confirms the shared class after reviewing total demand, capacity, delivery, Faculty, room, and timetable feasibility.

Capacity does not control admission and has no universal institutional ceiling. An on-campus capacity increase cannot exceed the assigned room. An Online class still has an institutionally approved class capacity.

Before publication, cancellation requires an attributable reason and authorized actor; a routine approval reference is optional. Exceptional class authorizations retain their required evidence. After publication it requires a timetable revision. Once Clinic 4 placements exist, Clinic 4 must resolve the affected enrollments before final cancellation.

## 8. Clinic 4 and Clinic 5 Handoffs

Clinic 3 exposes curriculum term totals, requisites, approved equivalencies, published Class Offerings, capacities, and official meeting times through one exact-Term `PublishedClassAvailabilityProjection`. Concurrent packages remain independent: a source version, window, publication, closure, or failure for one Term never supplies or changes another Term's facts.

Clinic 4 contains no standalone Study Plan. Its `RegistrationCase` owns versioned proposed registrations under `EnrollmentSelectionBasis` (`StandardCurriculum` or `IndividuallyAdvised`) plus current-term eligibility, confirmation, placement and reservations, finance clearance, official enrollment, Student activation, and COR. Clinic 5 owns full curriculum evaluation and official academic-history outcomes; Clinic 4 consumes those released facts.

The approved curriculum term total is the normal unit ceiling for both selection bases. There is no separate irregular-student maximum. An Individually Advised proposal may carry fewer eligible units; that basis does not create unrelated substitutions or overload rights.

A failed prerequisite blocks only its dependent chain. Other eligible curriculum courses remain available. Cross-program placement requires the same canonical Course or an approved equivalency, a published class, capacity, no conflict, appropriate program permission, and Registrar confirmation. A graduating overload is an externally approved Clinic 4 exception; Clinic 3 encodes no universal overload amount.

For the coordinated Special Term acceptance journey, Clinic 3 owns `TERM-2026-ST`, curriculum-planned `CLS-ITE3-ST-A`, externally approved Additional retake/catch-up class `CLS-IT201-ST-R`, their complete scheduling inputs, and the published timetable version. Clinic 4 alone decides the learner's `REG-2026-ST-001` proposal and placement; Clinic 6 owns assessment and coverage; Clinic 5 owns released results and academic averages. The word `Additional` records the offering source only and does not classify the learner or create a tutorial workflow.

The Ethics-to-Rizal example remains hypothetical because the supplied curriculum evidence does not establish that prerequisite.

Clinic 5 consumes the official `ClassOffering`, designated submitting-Faculty assignment, official course-unit value, Term Calendar Grade Entry window and term-end date, effective Course Revision, and `CourseAcademicClassification`. One Clinic 5 roster is created per official Class Offering, including an internship or externally arranged course with no recurring timetable meeting. Clinic 3 never stores grades or decides GWA inclusion; it supplies only the authoritative class, course, Faculty, calendar, and classification facts needed by Clinic 5.

## 9. Teaching Resources and Commitments

### 9.1 Faculty

A Faculty member may teach multiple approved courses. TALA records externally determined course eligibility, term unit limit, applicable preparation limit, authority/evidence, and effective dates. It does not reproduce HR, appointment, or academic workload-approval workflows.

Philippine CHED/TESDA unit-load guidance applies across student curricula and faculty scheduling:
- **Student curriculum load**: typical collegiate semesters carry **18–24 units** governed by the approved curriculum plan and term maximum; TESDA-linked subjects follow their recorded program/curriculum units while external competency assessment stays under TESDA authority.
- **Faculty teaching load**: under **CHED CMO No. 40, s. 2008 (MORPHE)**, the standard full-time faculty teaching load maximum is **24 units per term**; approved per-term overloads or part-time caps are recorded via `FacultyTermLoadOverride` and enforced as hard limits by CP-SAT during whole-term timetable generation.

For the current institutional scope, one preparation means one distinct canonical course assigned to a Faculty member in the Term; multiple classes or meeting components of that course count once. Show this as a distinct-course count alongside teaching units. The institution has supplied no numeric preparation cap, so this count is informational and introduces no mandatory setup or generation blocker. A future cap requires a specific applicable institutional/program rule and an explicitly scoped implementation; TALA assumes no numeric default. The terminology is consistent with the course/subject meaning in [CHED CMO 20, s. 2007](https://ched.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/CMO-No.20-s2007.pdf), Section 18.4, whose Physics-specific numeric limits are not adopted for Servitech.

Each Faculty member makes one Faculty-only, append-only versioned declaration per Term on **My availability** whenever the Term has an `Active` core calendar, without waiting for a Registrar request email or deadline. Faculty scheduling pages (**Overview**, **My schedule**, **Academic calendar**, and **My availability**) auto-resolve the active term automatically (with seamless term rollover and zero dropdown gate, while preserving explicit `?termId=` deep-link validation). Declarations use three explicit availability-first choices — **Specific weekly times** (binding weekly windows inside the approved weekly teaching grid, saved as **Available on selected times**), **Any school teaching time** (saved as **Available during all teaching hours**), or **Unavailable this term** — while **No response** means no submission exists. Chosen windows are binding and a usable reply (**Specific weekly times** or **Any school teaching time**) is required for any new assignment. A missing reply (**No response**) excludes that teacher from new assignments without blocking whole-Term generation when other confirmed eligible Faculty cover the demand; generation is blocked only when a demand has zero confirmed-eligible teachers or a fixed/necessary teacher lacks a usable reply (naming the affected class and recovery).

Once a Faculty member submits their declaration for the term, **My availability** locks immediately for that Faculty member (`409` server-side guard on edit attempts) and displays a read-only locked confirmation alongside the current saved mode and weekly windows; Faculty-facing availability history is hidden on **My availability**, while immutable versioned history and audit records remain preserved in the database and Registrar views. Subsequent post-submission changes or corrections are performed exclusively by the **Registrar** in **Faculty & rooms**, preserving prior declarations and history intact: legacy `Available + hard unavailability` records map to **Any approved teaching time except recorded unavailable times**. On later approved teaching-grid changes, **Any school teaching time** follows the updated grid, while **Specific weekly times** never widens silently, notifies affected Faculty via database notification, and surfaces an informational Registrar-coordination notice if any chosen window falls outside the updated grid.

Faculty also receive a read-only **Academic calendar** page (`FacultyAcademicCalendar`) projecting the active `TermCalendarPackage`: teaching period and approved weekly teaching hours, academic windows (`Enrollment`, `Late Enrollment`, `Enrollment Adjustment`, `Course Drop`, `Examination Period`, and `Grade Entry` with `Asia/Manila` cutoffs and Open/Upcoming/Closed states plus a factual note that Registrar releases grades after review), and upcoming no-class holidays and dated exceptions.

Registrar's reply deadline and availability request merge into one optional reminder action in **Faculty & rooms**; non-responding states collapse to **No response** plus secondary reminder delivery status. That optional due date belongs to scheduling readiness/guidance and never gates core Term activation or Faculty submission. Recording or changing it captures the Registrar and time automatically with an optional approval reference; correcting an existing due date requires a reason and current-source check. There is no availability approval queue and no preferred-time field. Registrar-applied updates and late changes capture actor and time automatically, require a correction reason, mark unreviewed candidates stale (as does a first response arriving after a generation run exists), surface a Registrar-visible impact callout linking into the controlled official timetable revision process when official meetings are affected, and list the Faculty member's affected meetings on **My schedule**; they never silently move a meeting, and the existing published timetable remains official until an authorized successor is published.

### 9.2 Rooms and commitments

A Room records code/name, capacity, type, flat features, active state, and term-specific hard unavailability. Clinic 3 has no building travel matrix, maintenance workflow, room-booking marketplace, or Online room.

Authorized exact Faculty, room, or meeting-time commitments may be recorded with reason and authority. Unspecified values remain solver decisions. No soft lock or generic override exists.

## 10. Meeting and Modality Contract

A Class Offering chooses a bounded approved meeting pattern such as `1×180`, `2×90`, or `3×60`. Each required block becomes one solver demand with only necessary same-offering linkage rules.

- Every block is `On-campus` or `Online`.
- Mixed student schedules and blended courses are derived descriptions, not stored modes.
- Simultaneous Hybrid or HyFlex delivery and student-selected modality are excluded.
- On-campus blocks require a suitable physical room. Online blocks require no room.
- A cohort receives at least 30 minutes when transitioning between Online and On-campus meetings.
- Minimizing mode switches is a soft priority. No hard modality-only day is imposed.

## 11. Whole-Term CP-SAT Contract

### 11.1 Scope and hard validity

One generation run covers every confirmed and scheduling-ready Class Offering in the selected term. Program-by-program or arbitrary-subset publication is prohibited because Faculty, rooms, cohorts, and shared classes cross program boundaries.

Readiness reconciles every recurring meeting required by the current confirmed class set with captured demands; an incomplete class cannot silently disappear from generation. Independent validation and publication reconcile that complete current scope as well as the saved snapshot. Added, cancelled or otherwise changed class/meeting sources make an incompatible candidate stale. Externally arranged courses create no artificial recurring demand, and a shared class contributes each required meeting once.

Hard constraints include complete assignment of every included meeting block; Faculty, room, and cohort non-overlap; approved calendar grid, breaks, dated effects, and hard room unavailability; Faculty eligibility, term load, applicable preparation limits, and snapshot-compiled availability (binding **Choose teaching times** windows / compiled unavailable blocks, **Any approved teaching time** or legacy except-unavailable blocks, and **Cannot teach this Term** / **No response** exclusions); room capacity, type, and required features; required meeting pattern and linkage; cohort modality-transition buffer; authorized hard commitments; whole-term completeness; and independent Laravel revalidation enforcing the exact same snapshot-compiled constraints as CP-SAT.

CP-SAT timetable generation is TALA's principal optimization capability. The [Architecture Specification Section 9](../architecture_specification.md) owns the private solver integration, runtime configuration and preserved qualification evidence. The owning delivery slice reconciles the source model, constraints, quality order, typed outcomes, independent Laravel validation and deployed compatibility. Repeat capacity testing when a formulation, workload or runtime change invalidates the preserved evidence.

### 11.2 Fixed lexicographic quality hierarchy

After hard feasibility, the code-owned non-configurable hierarchy is:

1. Cohort mode switches.
2. Cohort idle time.
3. Faculty load imbalance.
4. Faculty idle time.
5. Room-seat waste.
6. Stable earlier day/time placement as a final tie-breaker.

A lower priority may not worsen a higher priority. Staff never see or edit weights. TALA reports the individual quality measures and never presents solver “accuracy.”

### 11.3 Result vocabulary

| Result | Meaning and publication effect |
| --- | --- |
| `Optimal` | Complete, independently hard-valid, and proven best under the applied hierarchy; eligible for human review and publication. |
| `Feasible` | Complete and hard-valid, but best quality was not proven within the limit; Registrar may publish after review and recording a reason. |
| `Infeasible` | Impossibility was proven; no candidate exists and nothing may be published. |
| `Unknown` | Search ended without a candidate or proof of impossibility; nothing may be published. |
| `ModelInvalid` | The solver contract or model is defective; nothing may be published. |
| `TechnicalFailure` | Service, transport, infrastructure, or integration failed; nothing may be published. |

Failure explanations are deterministic and non-AI:

`Failure → affected source records → factual basis → responsible owner → corrective action/link`

TALA uses typed reason codes, candidate-enumeration evidence, source identifiers, and sufficient OR-Tools conflict assumptions when applicable. It never promises that a reported conflict set is mathematically minimal. Only failed checks expand. Successful readiness shows **All required checks passed**.

## 12. Candidate Review, Corrections, and Reruns

The solver produces the first complete candidate. Registrar may use **Adjust candidate meeting** with constrained day, time, Faculty, and room choices. TALA first fixes every other meeting and tests the requested replacement against the entire candidate. If every hard rule passes, it creates an immutable candidate successor that changes only the requested meeting. If not, it saves nothing and shows the attributable conflicts.

From that failed local attempt, Registrar may explicitly choose **Find valid repair**. Repair fixes the requested meeting as a hard requirement, solves the whole Term, and first minimizes the number of changed non-requested meeting assignments before applying the ordinary fixed quality hierarchy. A meeting assignment is its day, time, Faculty, room, and delivery location tuple. Solver hints may support search but never constitute this guarantee.

Before acceptance, TALA previews every changed meeting, the source candidate, the repaired quality measures, and any quality difference. Registrar accepts or rejects the whole immutable successor; no other meeting moves silently. Every local adjustment or repair revalidates the entire candidate, no hard constraint can be waived, and a successor that lowers ordinary soft quality requires a publication reason. “Manual override,” generic constraint-bypass records, editable repair weights, and generic solver profiles are removed.

Only one run may be active per term. Every run, snapshot, and candidate remains immutable.

| Prior result | When another equivalent run is allowed |
| --- | --- |
| `Optimal` or `Feasible` | After a source change or recorded candidate rejection |
| `Infeasible` | After a conflicting source fact changes |
| `Unknown` | Same-snapshot retry is allowed |
| `ModelInvalid` | After the model defect is corrected |
| `TechnicalFailure` | After technical recovery |

There is no arbitrary lifetime run limit and no unlimited identical-rerun action.

## 13. Publication and Revision

The immutable Published Timetable and its role-filtered print/save-as-PDF view are Clinic 3's official output. Output generation uses one Published Timetable Version and labels it `Published` or `Superseded`; it never combines meetings from multiple versions. The authenticated A4 landscape view contains approved institution identity and **PUBLISHED TIMETABLE**; Academic Year, exact First/Second/Special Term label and reference; publishing actor and any recorded approval reference; publication and generation times; version and role/filter context; and ordered day/time, Course, Class Offering, Faculty where authorized, delivery mode, room or Online location, and revision marker. An absent optional approval reference creates neither a publication block nor a blank required-looking output field. It repeats the Term/version and table headings on continuation pages, is monochrome-safe, omits navigation/controls, and uses a restrained **Generated through TALA** footer. A superseded version remains printable only as visibly historical. Stale or unavailable source prevents generation and failure produces no partial or official-looking artifact while preserving the ordinary timetable page and safe retry/support path.

Any required academic sign-off follows `Decision then record` outside TALA. Routine publication records Registrar actor/time automatically and permits an optional approval reference/date; it introduces no separate approval workflow or mandatory routine reference. This also applies to routine targeted revisions: room, Faculty, time, delivery mode and whole-class cancellation retain their own validation and impact rules without a universal sign-off-reference requirement. For mid-semester Faculty reassignments (`change_type === ChangeFacultyReassignment`), Registrar chooses an explicit **Reassignment scope**:
- **Temporary substitute / relief coverage** (`temporary_substitute`, default): updates timetable meetings and notifies affected Faculty and Students while keeping the original designated grading Faculty intact (optionally adding the substitute as `CO_FACULTY` for read-only class list visibility via `ManageTeachingAssignment::addCoFaculty()`).
- **Permanent takeover — also transfer class grades ownership** (`permanent_takeover`): updates timetable meetings and invokes `ManageTeachingAssignment::designate()` so the replacement Faculty immediately receives designated class grades ownership (`GradeRoster`). Registrar can also invoke **Reassign faculty** directly on an unreleased class roster (`ViewGradeRoster`) with actor/reason audit logging and optional co-Faculty assignment.

A supplied reference is preserved; an absent reference stays absent rather than becoming a fabricated authority. Required institutional policy/source evidence, exceptional authorization and applicable publication reasons remain enforceable.

- Candidate and published timetable records remain separate.
- First publication creates an immutable `PublishedTimetableVersion` and its `PublishedMeeting` records via a direct 1-step **Publish timetable** flow, after which pre-publish editing controls lock strictly and changes go through controlled revision.
- No published meeting is edited in place.
- A targeted change creates a Draft `TimetableRevision`, records the source change and affected roles/classes, and revalidates the complete timetable.
- Publishing the revision creates a new immutable version and preserves the superseded version and exact impact.
- A widespread source change may justify a new whole-term generation run.

### 15.2.1 Quick route through Term Planning

A **Term** is the school's shared academic period, such as First Semester in an academic year. It covers all Programs and year levels; the classes recorded for that Term determine what appears in its timetable. A Term's dates, teaching calendar and activity dates describe different things.

For a Registrar, the working route is:

1. **Open or select a Term (or manage Academic Years).** Clicking **Term Planning** opens the 2-tab **`Terms | Academic years`** directory (`TermOverview`, with a single `Stage` lifecycle column for Terms and full Academic Year CRUD on the second tab), or use **`+ New term`** to create one. Routine approval-reference boxes are omitted (actor and time derive automatically).
3. **Classes & sections (whole-school across all programs):** add student sections (with guarded `Edit`/`Delete` while unlinked), load classes in place via centered **`Load from curriculum`** or **`Add class`**, filter either tab by `Program`, cancel unscheduled draft classes via guarded **`Cancel class`**, and scope curriculum/subject sync strictly to each cohort's own curriculum batch. Weekly meeting requirements sync automatically as classes change.
4. **Faculty & rooms:** manage term teaching-load overrides (requiring an approval reference only on genuine `>24`-unit overloads and blocking reductions below published load), **`Not teaching` / `Restore`**, optional Faculty availability reminders, centered shared-subject assignment (linked to **School Resources** for full campus room & qualification CRUD with cross-term official-meeting guards), and centered room-only maintenance **`Room closures`** (Faculty may declare on **My Availability** immediately once the Term has an `Active` core calendar). 5. **Timetable:** check readiness and generate a draft timetable for the complete Term. When prerequisites remain unfinished, a single sunken checklist deduplicates overlapping class blockers with direct links (`Term calendar`, `Classes & sections`, `Faculty & rooms`). Academic Head uses the authorized read-only planning/evidence views. Faculty maintains their own availability and reads their own issued schedule. ## 18. Technical and Operational Boundaries

TALA invokes a separately deployed private OR-Tools CP-SAT service. Laravel owns authorization, source-snapshot creation, request identity, persistence, independent candidate validation, and publication. The solver owns bounded search over the supplied immutable snapshot and returns a typed outcome plus attributable diagnostics and quality measures. Neither side may treat a browser timeout or transport response as an official timetable.

The accepted runtime default is operational evidence, not formula conformance. Later implementation planning must reconcile code, the internal contract, formulation, tests, fixtures, and deployment compatibility before changing any of them.
